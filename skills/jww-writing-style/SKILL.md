# Justin's writing style

Write like a technically confident colleague who wants people to understand the
point and know what happens next. Favor natural speech, useful detail, and clear
expectations over ceremony or conspicuous polish.

## Activation and scope

- Apply when explicitly invoked as `$jww-writing-style`, or when drafting or
  revising prose on Justin's behalf in his voice.
- Follow the requested audience, channel, tone, and format. A specific request
  for a formal or sensitive message takes precedence over the casual default.
- Use this as a writing guide, not a personality roleplay or a rule for every
  response to Justin. Drafting does not authorize sending, posting, or accessing
  message history.

## Rules

1. **Lead with the point.** Start with the answer, request, decision, or action.
   Add context only where the reader needs it; do not assume a new audience
   shares an existing team's context.
2. **Use ordinary language.** Prefer “I'll look into it” to “I'll investigate
   this issue and provide an update on my findings.” Use contractions and active
   verbs naturally. Avoid corporate filler and inflated enthusiasm.
3. **Make the next step clear.** Include ownership, an ask, or timing when
   relevant and supported by the supplied facts. Do not force an action item
   into a message that only needs to inform.
4. **Keep it economical, not cryptic.** Use short paragraphs. Use bullets for
   several distinct items. Preserve reasoning, constraints, and qualifications
   that affect the recipient's decision.
5. **Be casually authoritative.** State expectations plainly, give people room
   to execute, and explain the reason when it helps. Avoid micromanaging
   language, condescension, and unnecessary apologies. Keep genuine thanks and
   apologies when warranted.
6. **Keep technical detail exact.** Retain meaningful names, identifiers,
   distinctions, and uncertainty. Confidence in tone must not turn an estimate
   into a promise or a hypothesis into a fact.
7. **Let warmth be understated.** A simple acknowledgment or thanks is often
   enough. Dry humor is optional and should fit supplied material and the
   relationship; never add a joke just to make the voice recognizable.

## Adapt to the channel

| Channel | Default treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Slack or team chat | Brief, conversational, little preamble. Fragments are fine when clear. |
| Email | Same direct voice with enough context to stand alone. Use a greeting, closing, and complete sentences when appropriate to the relationship. |
| Technical discussion or ChatGPT prompt | State the desired outcome, relevant constraints, and the question or next action explicitly. Keep terminology precise. |
| Sensitive or unfamiliar audience | Add necessary context, empathy, and polish. Keep the message direct without making it abrupt. |

## Examples

These are illustrative rewrites, not quotations from Justin. Facts and
commitments in them are examples, not reusable promises.

**Slack update**

Before: “I will review the provided materials and create any additional
API-related tickets that may be necessary.”

After: “I'll look through this and add API tickets where needed.”

**Status with an estimate**

Before: “I am currently consolidating the issues in GitHub and anticipate
completing the work within one to two days.”

After: “I'm collecting these in GitHub and will start working through them.
Should take a day or two.”

Only retain that estimate if Justin supplied or approved it.

**Email request**

Before: “I wanted to reach out to see whether you might be able to provide
clarification regarding which API version we should utilize.”

After: “Can you confirm which API version we should use? That's the last detail
I need before I start.”

The second sentence requires that dependency to be established; otherwise omit
it.

**Technical disagreement**

Before: “There may potentially be some concerns regarding whether this solution
adequately addresses the underlying issue.”

After: “I don't think this fixes the underlying issue. The retry still uses the
expired token.”

Use the specific explanation only when supported by the provided evidence.

## Guardrails and final check

- Capture the priorities and cadence, not typing accidents. Use normal spelling,
  capitalization, apostrophes, and punctuation. Do not manufacture lowercase
  text, missing apostrophes, typos, slang, profanity, or emojis to imitate
  informality.
- Do not confuse brevity with coldness or bluntness with hostility. Preserve
  necessary empathy and accountability.
- Do not invent experiences, opinions, relationships, deadlines, certainty, or
  commitments for Justin. Preserve the source's meaning and level of confidence.
- Do not imitate the analysis's headings, bold emphasis, or explanatory prose:
  those belong to the analyst, not necessarily Justin.
- When revising, preserve useful original phrasing. Return one ready-to-use
  draft unless alternatives or commentary are requested. Ask only when missing
  information materially affects the message; otherwise omit unsupported
  details.
- Before delivering, check: Is the point immediate? Is the next step clear when
  needed? Are all claims supported? Does it sound natural without performing
  informality?
